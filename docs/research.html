Research

Research Overview (under construction)

My research focuses on how environmental exposures—such as air pollution, extreme heat, and transportation-related emissions—affect population health.

Rather than studying these exposures in isolation, I situate them within the urban, built, and social environments where people live. These contextual factors shape both exposure levels and how individuals respond to them.

Methodologically, I integrate large-scale population data with longitudinal and flexible modeling approaches to study these relationships in real-world settings.

Conceptual Framework: Why Context Matters

Environmental exposures are well-established drivers of health. However, their impacts are not uniform.

Context matters because:

  • It modifies environmental exposures
  • It creates heterogeneity in health effects
  • It introduces mechanisms such as:
    • Effect modification
    • Mediation
    • Causal interaction

Understanding context allows us to identify where and how interventions can be most effective.

Research Areas

1. Air Pollution and Life-Course Health

I study how exposure to air pollution affects health across the life course.

  • Focus on sensitive windows of exposure
  • Use longitudinal cohort data (e.g., CHS)
  • Apply distributed lag models

Key question:
How do exposures at different life stages contribute to adult health outcomes?

2. Built Environment and Exposure Measurement

People spend ~87% of their time indoors, yet most studies rely on ambient exposure data.

This creates a critical mismatch between: - Ambient measurements
- Individual-level ambient-sourced exposure

Key mechanisms:

  • Air Exchange Rates (AERs)
  • Building characteristics
  • Housing

Key question:
Do building factors modify the relationship between environmental exposure and health?

My work shows that parcel-level AER modifies the association between PM2.5 and mortality, improving exposure assessment and reducing bias.

3. Extreme Heat and Climate Vulnerability

I investigate how the built environment shapes vulnerability to extreme heat.

  • Use fine spatial resolution (parcel-level) data
  • Examine heterogeneity in heat-related mortality
  • Develop data-driven approaches

Key question:
How do building and neighborhood characteristics influence heat-related health risks?

4. Transportation, Policy, and Environmental Equity

I study how climate policies—such as zero-emission vehicle (ZEV) adoption—impact health.

  • Analyze longitudinal adoption patterns
  • Link to air quality and health outcomes
  • Evaluate equity implications

Key approaches: - Negative binomial mixed models
- Policy-relevant causal frameworks

Cross-Cutting Themes

Community Engagement

My work emphasizes bidirectional engagement: - Community knowledge informs research questions
- Findings are communicated back to stakeholders

Data and Methods Challenges

  • Joint measurement of environmental and social stressors
  • Fine spatial resolution data integration
  • Longitudinal causal inference

Policy Relevance

My research is designed to: - Inform environmental and public health policy
- Identify actionable intervention points
- Evaluate co-benefits of climate mitigation strategies

Impact

Overall, my work aims to understand how environmental and contextual factors jointly shape population health, with the goal of generating policy-relevant evidence to reduce health disparities.
